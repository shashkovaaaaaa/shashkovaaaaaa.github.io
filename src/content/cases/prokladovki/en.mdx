<TextSection title="Project Context">

The marketplace connected storage owners with people looking to rent self-storage spaces. As supply grew, manually onboarding every new host became a bottleneck, creating the need for a scalable self-service onboarding experience.

<Column title="Audience">

The primary audience was storage owners who wanted to list and rent out their spaces through the marketplace. This included individuals, self-employed hosts, sole proprietors, and companies — each with different legal, verification, and documentation requirements.

</Column>

<Column title="Business Goals">

- Scale host acquisition without increasing the operational workload.
- Increase marketplace supply by helping more hosts create their first listing independently.

</Column>

<Column title="User Goals">

- Complete registration and verification with as little manual data entry as possible.
- Provide only the information and documents relevant to their legal status.
- Create and publish their first storage listing as quickly as possible.

</Column>

<Column title="Success Metrics">

Success would be measured by how effectively hosts could complete onboarding and reach their first published listing without manual support.

<BadgeList
  items={[
    'Host Activation Rate',
    'Onboarding Completion Rate',
    'Time to First Listing',
    'Manual Intervention Rate',
  ]}
/>

</Column>

Market context: The product was designed for the Russian market, where onboarding requirements vary by legal status and may include INN (TIN)-based business verification, local banking details, and a public offer agreement.

</TextSection>

<TextSection title="Learning from Existing Host Onboarding Patterns">

To understand how complex host onboarding could be simplified, I analyzed how established marketplaces guide suppliers through registration, verification, payout setup, and the creation of their first listing.

Rather than copying a specific flow, I focused on recurring patterns that could be adapted to the legal and operational requirements of a self-storage marketplace.

<TextSection title="Mapping the Universal Host Onboarding Flow">

Based on the recurring patterns identified during competitive research, I mapped a universal host onboarding flow covering the full journey from registration to completing the first storage listing.

The goal was to create a clear baseline that captured all essential steps before identifying friction points and exploring how the experience could be improved.

<TextSection title="Turning Friction into Product Hypotheses">

The universal flow exposed several points where hosts were likely to drop off: too much manual data entry, different requirements for each legal status, and a long path before reaching the first published listing.

I translated these friction points into three product hypotheses and prioritized them based on expected user impact, business value, and implementation complexity.

<Columns>
<Column title="Adaptive Onboarding">

If onboarding branches immediately after legal status selection, each host will only go through the steps and requirements relevant to them

</Column>
<Column title="Automation-First Data Collection">

If available data is automatically extracted from identity documents or retrieved by Tax ID, users will spend less time filling out forms and make fewer input errors

</Column>
<Column title="Activation-Focused Onboarding">

If onboarding naturally continues from verification to creating the first storage unit and signing the offer, more hosts will reach a listing ready for publication

</Column>
</Columns>

The prioritization showed that adaptive onboarding and an activation-focused flow could deliver strong impact with relatively manageable complexity.

Automation also had high potential, but depended on third-party integrations, so I treated it as an enhancement layer rather than making the entire flow dependent on it.

</TextSection>

<TextSection title="What the Final Concept Solves">

The final onboarding concept addressed three core challenges:

- Less irrelevant complexity – the flow adapts to the host’s legal status.
- Less manual work – reliable data is retrieved or extracted automatically.
- A clear path to activation – registration ends with the first usable listing, not an empty account.

</TextSection>

<TextSection title="Recommendations & Next Steps">

The next step would be to validate whether the new flow actually reduces friction without increasing operational risk. I would focus on where users drop off, how reliably automation performs, and whether more hosts reach a listing ready for publication without manual support.

<Callout title="Reflection">

This project reinforced one of my core product design beliefs: constraints do not necessarily make a journey harder. When they are introduced at the right moment, they can give users clarity, confidence, and a sense of progress.

Good onboarding works like a guided tour. It does not simply explain the product — it leads users toward their first meaningful outcome. In this case, that meant not just creating an account, but getting the first storage unit ready to go live.

</Callout>
